# Similarity vs MMR

## 1. The Trap of Redundancy

User asks: *"Tell me about the iPhone 15 camera."*

**Similarity Search (k=4) returns:**
1.  iPhone 15 has a 48MP camera.
2.  The camera on iPhone 15 is 48MP.
3.  The iPhone 15 features a 48MP sensor.
4.  48MP is the resolution of the iPhone 15.

The LLM gets 4 versions of the same sentence. It misses info about "Night Mode," "Zoom," or "Video."
This is **Redundancy Collapse**.

## 2. Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR)

MMR tries to maximize **Relevance** (Similarity) while punishing **Redundancy** (Similarity to already selected docs).

Algorithm intuition:
1.  Find the most similar doc (Doc A). Select it.
2.  Find the next doc that is similar to Query BUT dissimilar to Doc A.
3.  Repeat.

## 3. The Result

**MMR (k=4) returns:**
1.  iPhone 15 has a 48MP camera. (High Relevance)
2.  Night Mode is improved by 2x. (High Relevance, Low overlap with #1)
3.  Video supports 4K60. (High Relevance, Low overlap with #1, #2)
4.  The new Telephoto lens is 5x. (High Relevance, Low overlap with #1, #2, #3)

## 4. Summary

If your RAG answers feel repetitive or shallow, switch to MMR.
It forces the retriever to explore the "Concept Space" rather than getting stuck in one corner.

> **Key Intuition:** "Don't tell me the same thing 5 times."
